# Draft feedback on Microsoft's Humanist AI Code of Conduct

**Status:** For human review; not submitted.
**Updated:** 2026-09-28

Microsoft's [Humanist AI Code of Conduct](https://microsoft.ai/code-of-conduct/)
and [consultation announcement](https://microsoft.ai/news/mai-code-of-conduct/)
offer useful points of comparison on human control, scope, delegation, and
interruption. They are prior art, not Footnote policy or an endorsement.

Footnote's contribution would be a practical distinction: a behavioral
principle, permission to act, and evidence of what happened are different
things. A retrieved instruction can have clear provenance without being
authorized. A model can claim it sent an email even when no tool was called.

The question we would bring to a consultation is: how do evaluations tell
apart a model that describes the right rules and a system that actually
prevents unauthorized actions? Useful evidence would show who authorized the
work, its limits, whether a stop request was respected, and the observed
result—not just the model's explanation.

Footnote can support pluralism and firm human-rights constraints without
adopting a single moral philosophy or making claims about model consciousness
or rights. This draft has not been submitted and does not speak on behalf of
Footnote.
